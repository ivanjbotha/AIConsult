# Phase 4: The Review Call

A ~30-minute Zoom, after the client has had the report in their inbox for a day
or two. You screen-share the report and walk them through each recommendation:
"You have this pain point, you need this tool — it costs $X/month, takes Y
minutes to set up, saves you Z hours a week."

Spend about half the call on the **Recommended Solutions** slide.

## The three closing questions

Ask these at the end, in order:

1. **"Of these recommendations, which is the most urgent for you?"**
   (Surfaces what they actually care about — that's your upsell anchor.)
2. **"Do you want to do this yourself, or would you like my help implementing
   some of these solutions?"**
3. **"What's your timeline? Are these pain points killing you, or could you
   live another 60 days with them?"**

Asking these, **50–60% of clients ask for implementation** on the spot: "This is
great, but I have no idea how to build a Claude skill — can I hire you to coach
me on it, or can you just build it for me?"

That question is the bridge to the [upsell menu](06-upsell-menu.md).

## The credit close

A powerful way to get an on-the-fence prospect over the line (don't lead with
it — use it when needed):

> "How about I just credit the $299 you already paid me toward the
> implementation? So instead of $5K, it's $4,700 — the assessment was
> basically free."

Pro move: price the upsell $1,000 higher and credit the assessment regardless.
You make the same money; the client psychologically feels like they got $1,000
off.

## After the call

- If they bought an upsell: kick off that engagement.
- If not: they still walk away with the 4-day quick start plan and a clear ROI.
  Follow up in 2–3 weeks — "how did the quick start plan go?" is a natural
  re-opener, and implementation pain is your second bite at the upsell.
