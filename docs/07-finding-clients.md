# Seven Ways to Find Clients (No Capital, No Audience)

The most common question by far: "sounds great, but how do I find customers if
I have no audience and no money?" Seven methods. None require capital or an
audience. Most people won't do them for more than 3 days — hang in for 7 and
you're in the top 0.1%.

Copy-paste scripts for every method:
[templates/outreach-messages.md](../templates/outreach-messages.md).

## 1. Host a local "AI for Business" meetup

- Barter with a co-working space: "I'll bring 30 local business owners into
  your space for exposure — can I have your conference room for an hour, free?"
- You organized it, so you're automatically perceived as the expert.
- Format: 10–15 min networking → **20-minute presentation on how to use
  Claude** (simple enough to understand, advanced enough to wow) → work the
  room, collect contact info at the door, follow up within 24 hours offering
  the assessment.
- Think of it like SEO: the first event might not land a client; events 3–6
  will. Run it monthly and build a local event brand where you're the leader.

## 2. Door knocking

The single best way to learn sales, period. Results are immediate — need a
client *today*? Knock a door. Real numbers from someone who actually did it:
30 local service businesses visited, asked for the manager/owner → 5 meetings
→ **2 clients**. Out of ~150,000 podcast listeners who heard the method, he was
the only one who did it. That's the competition level.

## 3. LinkedIn DMs (the non-spam kind)

Target local business owners in your city. **Never pitch in message one** —
that's the fastest way to fail. Probe about pain and how they're currently
using AI:

> "Hey Joe, my name's Corey — I probably live down the street from you. Very
> curious how you're using AI in your business. Would love to give you some
> free feedback on ways I think you could implement AI."

"I probably live down the street from you" is a great hook. Voice messages and
30-second Loom/phone videos work even better — 99% of people won't do them, so
competition is near zero.

## 4. Free mini-audits for your network

Everyone knows other business owners — gym, church, kids' school, friends. One
text:

> "I'm getting into the AI services world. I'd love to spend 15 minutes with
> you, completely free, and show you one way AI could benefit your business.
> Open to that?"

The mini-audit is the lead magnet; the full $999 assessment is the upsell.
Frame it as no-lose: "Worst case, you learn one or two tools you'd never heard
of. Best case, you've got a partner who can build all the AI stuff that's been
sitting on your to-do list."

## 5. Agency & professional partners

Your insurance agent, accountant, marketing agency, coach — their clients are
your ideal clients, and those clients are asking them AI questions they can't
answer (which makes them look bad). Offer to be their answer:

> "I'm in the AI space. I'd love to help any of your clients with AI-related
> questions — happy to send a referral fee for anyone who becomes a client."

**The key is follow-up.** You won't get an A1 referral partner from one
attempt. Ping every 2–3 weeks: "Any of your clients asking about AI lately?
Happy to act as a resource." Do that consistently and referrals come.

Level up: run a **co-branded workshop** ("AI for tax season — brought to you by
[you] × [the accounting firm]"). They stay top of mind with their customers;
you get access to their customer base. Combine with method 1: partner with a
plugged-in local realtor, run the meetup in their office for their clients and
colleagues — that exact play produced a client at the first event.

## 6. AI office hours at a co-working space

> "I'd love to come into your space once a week for an hour and offer myself as
> a free AI resource to your tenants — they can walk up and ask me anything."

Win-win-win: the space gets a value-add for tenants, tenants get free AI
consulting, and you're sitting directly inside your ICP. Do a good job and
they'll want to hire you.

Beast mode (a real example): pitch ~10 co-working spaces (2 will say yes), then
door-knock local businesses for free gift cards to give away at the session
($175 collected). Result: 11 attendees at the first session, 2 follow-up calls
booked.

## 7. Post your wins (build in public)

Document as you go — short videos, X posts, carousels. A "win" is smaller than
you think: someone texted you a ChatGPT question and you gave a great answer?
That's a post ("here was their question, here's what I said").

Early on you won't have many wins, so manufacture consistent value for your
ICP instead: e.g., "the one ChatGPT prompt you need to see today," every day
for 90 days. By the end of the 90 days you'll have real results from real
assessments to layer on top.

---

## Combining methods

The methods compound: partners × meetups (method 5 + 1), door knocking ×
office hours (2 + 6), mini-audits feeding build-in-public content (4 + 7).
Pick two, run them for a month, and follow up relentlessly.
