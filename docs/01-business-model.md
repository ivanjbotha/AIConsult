# The Business Model: AI Tools Assessment

## The offer

You sit down with a small business owner for 45 minutes and run a structured
interview that pulls out the bottlenecks and pain points in their business. Based
on that conversation, you prescribe **3–7 off-the-shelf AI tools** they can
implement immediately to reclaim **5–10 hours per week**.

**The guarantee:** if you can't find at least 5 hours per week of opportunity
where AI can help, they get 100% of their money back. The only thing the client
has to lose is 45 minutes of their time. Best case, they get at least 5 hours a
week back — the average client saves about 7.

**Price: $299.**

## Why this works

Every business owner you talk to about AI falls into one of two camps:

1. **Too busy in the weeds.** They work 40–60 hours a week and have no time to
   learn this stuff.
2. **Afraid and avoiding it.** They know competitors are getting ahead, so they
   bury their head in the sand.

Maybe 5% of companies use any AI tool beyond prompting ChatGPT. There are
millions of small businesses. This is a blue ocean — even if everyone reading
this playbook implemented it, there would be opportunity left over.

## Why it's accessible

- **No audience required.** Seven acquisition methods in
  [07-finding-clients.md](07-finding-clients.md) need zero followers.
- **No capital required.** Everything here starts from nothing.
- **No coding required.** You're not building automations or writing code.
  You're *prescribing tools that already exist* — like a doctor writing a
  prescription. A doctor who needs no qualifications.

The one thing you do need is a working understanding of AI tools. That's what
the research phase (and the tool directories in
[03-phase-2-ai-analysis.md](03-phase-2-ai-analysis.md)) gives you.

## The ideal client

| Attribute | Sweet spot |
|-----------|-----------|
| Employees | 2–20 |
| Revenue | $500K–$5M/year |
| Profile | Owner-operator, working in the weeds, knows they're behind on AI |

## The four-phase fulfillment flow

1. **Discovery call** (45 min, recorded) — probe for pain, never pitch.
2. **AI analysis** — feed the transcript to Claude; it finds patterns you missed
   and researches tools that fix each pain point. You QA the output.
3. **The report** — a stupid-simple, client-facing deliverable generated from
   the template in `templates/report/`.
4. **Review call** (30 min) — walk through the report, ask the three closing
   questions, and let the upsell come to you.

## $299 is just the door

Some people hear the price and think "that's it?" The assessment is a foot in
the door — a tripwire offer. The lifetime value of an assessment client ends up
being $3K, $5K, $10K or more, because:

- **~50–60% of assessment clients ask for implementation help** on the review call.
- The report itself tees up upsells (the "What Comes After Quick Wins" slide).
- **Your client is literally paying you to uncover opportunities for them to
  pay you more.** That's what a good audit does.

Once the system is solid, the assessment can even become a self-liquidating
funnel: put $60 of each $299 into ads and you're getting paid to acquire
customers for your higher-ticket services.

## Make it yours

Don't stop at copying the model — niche it down:

- **Geographic:** be "the AI guy/gal in [your city]" (in-person meetups make
  this real fast).
- **Vertical:** if you have 10 years in financial services, be "the person who
  does AI assessments for financial services."

Niching lets you charge more and makes you far less prone to pricing pressure —
as long as you have the expertise to back it up.
